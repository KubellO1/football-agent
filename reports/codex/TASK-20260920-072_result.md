## Rate-limit findings

The previous 30-request Canary ran in roughly 12 seconds; 12 responses were HTTP 200 with `errors.rateLimit`. Its per-request timestamps and headers were not preserved, so a burst is the most consistent explanation, **not a proven exclusive cause**. Shared IP/account traffic remains possible. The official [API-Football rate-limit guidance](https://www.api-football.com/news/post/how-ratelimit-works) describes the Mega 900/minute limit, 15/second per-IP ceiling, daily quotas and HTTP 429. The [official optimization guidance](https://www.api-football.com/news/post/how-to-optimize-api-sports-calls-and-quota-usage) explicitly shows `errors.rateLimit` in an HTTP-200 body. No endpoint-specific ceiling was established from docs or this sample.

The task branch adds a shared, process-local API-Football admission limiter: serialized requests, configurable minimum spacing (default 2 s), local 20/minute, 1,000/day, 100/run budgets; provider-reported headers can tighten those caps. Both 429 and HTTP-200 body-level `rateLimit` are failures, with bounded retries, reset/`Retry-After` handling and a circuit breaker. Injury fetches no longer turn an application error into an empty injury result. HTTP error logs no longer include response bodies or exception URLs. This is **not** a cross-process quota ledger; concurrent writers would need one before rollout.

## Slow Canary

Phase 3 made 15 deliberately paced requests: five-league fixtures, five completed-fixture statistics, two completed-fixture lineups, two upcoming-fixture injuries and one standings response. Phase 4, permitted only after zero rateLimit responses, made 10 more: bookmaker catalog, Match Winner bet catalog and eight naturally upcoming fixture odds requests. Total **25/25 HTTP 200**, zero body `rateLimit`, zero 429, zero 403, zero retries. Observed account headers: 150,000/day, 900/minute; after the last request, daily remaining 149,975 and minute remaining 883. The sanitized per-request evidence is `TASK-20260920-072_request_timeline.json`.

Fixture clock: 5/5 leagues, 124/124 returned fixtures with exact kickoff. Injuries: two sampled fixtures returned 18 records each. Lineups: both sampled completed fixtures supplied both starting XIs; this does not prove T-90 availability. Real xG: only one of five sampled completed fixtures supplied xG for both teams. Standings: the Premier League sample returned 20 teams; four other leagues were not retested in this paced run.

Eight upcoming fixtures across all five leagues returned **69 complete named-bookmaker Match Winner/1X2 price triples**. Bet365 and Pinnacle appeared on 8/8; Betfair 7/8; William Hill 8/8. The live names also included Marathonbet, BetVictor, SBO, 1xBet and Betano. Unibet was in the bookmaker catalog but not in any of the eight actual fixture responses. The bet catalog established Match Winner ID 1; it was not guessed. Each sample records fixture ID, league, kickoff, bookmaker ID/name, three decimal prices, captured time and the provider's top-level update time in `TASK-20260920-072_odds_canary.json`.

**Critical blocker:** provider update times were 169–485 minutes older than collection for the eight sampled fixtures. None met the existing 30-minute quote-freshness rule. The API shows named 1X2 capability, but the sample does **not** establish production-quality fresh odds for formal decisions. `captured_at` must never replace an older provider timestamp to make a stale quote appear fresh. Checkpoint polling may build our own append-only point-in-time archive from now forward; it cannot create historical provider timestamps, and provider odds updates are not necessarily synchronized to T-90/T-60/T-30.

## Request economics

Planning estimate, not observed job traffic: five league fixture refreshes plus five standings calls = 10 baseline/day. For each match: T-24 and T-6 odds + injuries (4), T-90/T-60/T-30 odds + lineups (6), post-match statistics (1), for `10 + 11 × matches/day`. At 3.05 matches/day average: about **44/day** (0.0293% of 150,000). A 20-match day: about **230/day** (0.1533%). Per-run, per-minute, source update cadence and other account consumers matter more than the nominal daily quota. These calls are on the existing Mega subscription; no purchase or plan change was made.
